Why It Happens

Hindi lang "bad decision." Mas komplikado kaysa dun.

Gender Norms & Double Standards

Sa Pilipinas, may double standards kapag dating sa sexuality:

  • Ang boys are "encouraged" na mag-explore, pero ang girls are "expected" na magpigil
  • Ang girls who get pregnant ay "disgrasya," pero ang boys ay hindi blamed
  • Ang consent ay hindi always clear dahil sa power imbalance
  • Ang rape at sexual abuse ay under-reported dahil sa stigma[5]

Ang gender norms na ito ay nagiging dahilan din na hindi girls ang lumalaban sa pressure at hindi boys ang nag-reresponsibilidad.

Poverty & Limited Opportunities

Ang teenage pregnancy ay strongly linked sa poverty[6]:

  • Poor families have limited access sa health services at information
  • Sa poor communities, early marriage/union ay minsan nakikita na "solution" sa financial problems
  • Limited educational opportunities mean fewer alternatives
  • 10.3% ng poorest quintile vs 1.8% ng wealthiest quintile[1]

NEDA estimated na ang teenage pregnancy costs the Philippines 24-42 billion pesos in lost lifetime earnings[6].

Lack of Information & Services

Maraming kabataan ang hindi alam ang basics:

  • Only 24% ng males at 14% ng females used protection sa first sexual encounter[6]
  • Only 12% ng males at 18% ng females knew when a woman is most likely to get pregnant[6]
  • Maraming schools ay hindi fully implementing CSE dahil sa cultural resistance[7]
  • Health providers minsan refuse to serve adolescents dahil sa personal beliefs[7]
Myth: Talking about sex makes teens do it Fact: Research shows CSE delays sexual initiation and increases protection use[8]

Child Marriage & Sexual Abuse

⚠️ Content Notice: This section discusses abuse and coercion.

Ang very young mothers (10-14 years old) ay victims of abuse, hindi自愿 na choices[2]:

  • Noong 2023, 3,343 live births sa mothers aged 9-14[2]
  • Only 21 fathers were of similar age sa 3,343 mothers aged 9-14[2]
  • RA 11596 (2021) now bans child marriage, pero cultural practices persist
  • RA 11648 (2022) raised age of sexual consent to 16[9]

Ang data na ito ay indicative ng statutory rape at abuse. Hindi ito "teenage relationships."

Stigma & Shame

Ang stigma ay nagpapahirap sa mga kabataan na:

  • Magseek help sa health services
  • Ipatuloy ang pag-aaral
  • Makausap ang family openly
  • Access contraception

Ang stigma din ay disproportionately affects girls, while boys often escape accountability.

The Role of Teen Fathers

Ang story ng teenage pregnancy ay hindi lang about mothers. Ang teen fathers din ay part ng picture:

  • Minsan nag-aabandon ang partners
  • Minsan denied responsibility dahil sa "boys will be boys" mentality
  • May emotional at financial burden din silang hinaharap
  • Need support at education din sa parenting at responsibility

Ang teen fathers need support din — hindi lang blame. Responsibility is shared.

Effects on Life

Health

  • Higher risk ng maternal complications[3]
  • Higher risk ng infant mortality at low birth weight[3]
  • Mental health challenges (depression, anxiety)

Education

  • School dropout — DeEd Order 37 s. 2022 protects right to stay in school[10]
  • Limited future opportunities
  • Intergenerational cycle of poverty[6]
Education shouldn't end because of pregnancy.

Income

  • Lower lifetime earnings[6]
  • Economic dependence sa family or partner

The Myth About Sex Education

"Talking about sex makes teens do it"

This is FALSE.

Research shows that comprehensive sexuality education (CSE) actually:

  • Delays sexual initiation[8]
  • Increases condom and contraceptive use[8]
  • Reduces rates of teenage pregnancy[8]
  • Reduces STI rates[8]

Source: UNESCO, UNFPA, WHO (2018)[8]

Summary

Ang teenage pregnancy ay caused by:

  • Gender norms na unfair sa girls
  • Poverty at limited opportunities
  • Lack of accurate information at services
  • Child marriage at abuse (sa very young)
  • Stigma na nagpapahirap magseek help

Hindi ito blame game. Ang solution ay systemic change, hindi shaming.
