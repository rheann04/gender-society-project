Know the Law

Ang batas ay nandiyan para protektahan ka. Alamin mo.

I-click ang tabs sa ibaba para makita ang mga batas at policies na may kinalaman sa teenage pregnancy, reproductive health, at protection ng kabataan.

RA 10354 (2012)

Responsible Parenthood and Reproductive Health Act

What it says:

  • Provides universal access sa reproductive health care services
  • Mandates age- and development-appropriate reproductive health education sa schools[11]
  • Guarantees access sa medically-safe, non-abortifacient contraceptives
  • Prohibits discrimination based sa reproductive health decisions

Who it protects:

Everyone, including adolescents, especially poor women at those in remote areas.

What it means for teenagers:

You have the right to accurate information about reproductive health at sexuality education. You can access family planning services (with parental guidance for minors).

Key provisions for teens:

  • Section 14: Age- and development-appropriate RH education[11]
  • Section 23: Prohibits withholding of RH information

Source: Official Gazette of the Philippines[11]

RA 7610 (1992)

Special Protection of Children Against Abuse, Exploitation and Discrimination Act

What it says:

  • Protects children from all forms of abuse, including sexual abuse
  • Criminalizes child abuse and exploitation
  • Mandates reporting of child abuse cases

Who it protects:

All children (persons below 18 years old).

What it means for teenagers:

If you are being abused, exploited, or coerced, this law protects you. Adults who abuse children face criminal penalties.

Penalties:

Imprisonment and fines depending on the gravity of the abuse.

Source: Lawphil[12]

RA 8353 (1997)

Anti-Rape Law of 1997

What it says:

  • Reclassified rape as a crime against persons (not against chastity)
  • Expanded definition of rape
  • Removed the requirement that the victim must "resist to the utmost"

Who it protects:

All persons, especially women and children.

Source: Lawphil[13]

RA 11648 (2022)

Raised Age of Sexual Consent to 16

What it says:

  • Raised the age of sexual consent from 12 to 16 years old[9]
  • Sexual activity with anyone under 16 is statutory rape
  • Exception: No criminal liability if age difference is ≤3 years, consensual, non-abusive, non-exploitative (but NOT if victim is under 13)[9]

Who it protects:

Children and adolescents under 16 years old from sexual exploitation and abuse.

What it means for teenagers:

Anyone under 16 cannot legally consent to sex. Sexual activity with them is considered rape, with narrow exceptions for close-in-age relationships (but never for under 13).

Penalties:

Rape is punishable by reclusion perpetua (20-40 years) or reclusion temporal (12-20 years), depending on circumstances.

Source: Official Gazette[9]

RA 11596 (2021)

Prohibiting Child Marriage

What it says:

  • Prohibits child marriage (marriage before 18 years old)[14]
  • Declares child betrothal and marriage as having no legal effect[14]
  • Criminalizes facilitation of child marriage

Who it protects:

All children under 18 years old, especially girls.

What it means for teenagers:

You cannot legally marry before 18. Any marriage before 18 is void. Adults who arrange child marriage face criminal penalties.

Penalties:

  • Prision mayor (6-12 years) and fine of P40,000[14]
  • If perpetrator is parent/guardian: Prision mayor in maximum period, fine of P50,000, and perpetual loss of parental authority[14]

Source: Supreme Court E-Library[14]

RA 9710 (2009)

Magna Carta of Women

What it says:

  • Prohibits expulsion or non-readmission of female students due to pregnancy outside of marriage[15]
  • Prohibits refusing admission solely because of pregnancy[15]

Who it protects:

Female students and faculty who become pregnant outside of marriage.

What it means for teenagers:

Schools cannot kick you out or refuse to admit you just because you're pregnant. You have the right to continue your education.

Source: Philippine Commission on Women[15]

DepEd Order No. 31, s. 2018

Comprehensive Sexuality Education Policy

What it says:

  • Establishes policy guidelines for implementing CSE in all schools[12]
  • CSE is age-appropriate, culturally relevant, scientifically accurate[12]
  • Applies to all learners in public and private schools[12]

What it means for teenagers:

You should receive comprehensive sexuality education in school that is accurate and appropriate for your age.

Source: Department of Education[12]

Executive Order No. 141 (2021)

Teenage Pregnancy Prevention as National Priority

What it says:

  • Adopts prevention of teenage pregnancies as a National Priority[16]
  • Mobilizes government agencies to address root causes[16]
  • Recognizes gender norms, lack of information, and discrimination as root causes[16]
  • Strengthens adolescents' capacity to make informed decisions[16]

What it means for teenagers:

The government is required to coordinate efforts to prevent teenage pregnancy through education, health services, and addressing gender norms.

Source: Official Gazette[16]

What You Can Do

Know your rights. Ang batas ay nandiyan para protektahan ka, hindi parusahan ka.

  • If you're pregnant, you have the right to stay in school (RA 9710)
  • If you're under 16 and someone is pressuring you for sex, that's illegal (RA 11648)
  • If you're being abused, you can report it (RA 7610)
  • You have the right to accurate RH information (RA 10354)

For legal advice, consult with a lawyer or reach out to organizations like the Public Attorney's Office (PAO) or the Commission on Human Rights (CHR).
